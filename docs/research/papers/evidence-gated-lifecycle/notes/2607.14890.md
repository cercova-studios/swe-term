## Narrow claim

The paper claims that a lifecycle transition can be gated by fresh,
tracked-source-state-bound, mechanically verifiable evidence, treating agent
outputs as claims rather than lifecycle state. Its abstract reports mechanism,
tamper, and ablation tests, but limits the evaluation to one model family, 24
ablation tasks, and a self-hosted corpus.

## Mechanism

- Treatment or causal change: require a gate-admissible receipt tied to current
  tracked source state before accepting a lifecycle claim.
- Control or comparator: accept a success or review result without an equivalent
  current-state identity gate.
- Enforcement point: deterministic lifecycle state transition.
- Dependencies: the paper's full system includes an unattended loop and review
  machinery; neither is required for the narrow receipt mechanism.

## Evidence

- Paper-reported mechanism checks: 10 unattended-loop scenarios and 18 receipt
  tamper classes.
- Paper-reported ablation: a 9,240-cell, pre-registered comparison; the abstract
  attributes a near-compute difference to enforcing review as a lifecycle gate.
- Evaluator boundary: the paper's local-key receipt checks are most relevant to
  this experiment; broader loop outcomes remain model- and corpus-dependent.
- Negative evidence: the paper explicitly limits its evaluation to one model
  family, 24 ablation tasks, and a self-hosted corpus.

## Evaluator audit

- Measurement type: mixed — mechanical receipt/tamper checks plus model-mediated
  loop and ablation evaluation.
- Independence from the system under test: local receipt validation is a stronger
  signal than self-application outcomes, but still comes from one implementation.
- Rubric or verifier identity: not transferable as-is; swe-term will define a
  closed, deterministic identity comparison and explicit rule IDs.
- Known disagreement or circularity: no independent replication is established
  here; headline loop results must not be used to justify local architecture.

## Boundary conditions and limitations

The transfer is an inference: a receipt identity can prevent stale lifecycle
promotion in swe-term if every relevant verification input is represented and
refreshed. It cannot establish semantic program correctness, replace scoped test
selection, or decide policy. New inputs omitted from the identity remain a
possible stale-evidence failure.

## swe-term transfer

- Observed local failure: a previously passing result can be used after a
  relevant source or verifier input changes.
- Relevant contracts: `VerificationReceipt`, `Obligation`, lifecycle invariant 6,
  and the target state-expansion refactor.
- Smallest transplantable mechanism: pure receipt gate comparing a sealed
  passing receipt identity to the current obligation identity.
- What not to copy: unattended-loop engine, reviewer topology, local-key bundle
  format, self-application corpus, and model-dependent outcome claims.
- New risk: an incomplete identity falsely treats a changed environment as fresh;
  explicit fields and fail-closed validation are required.

## Cheapest falsification

- Hypothesis: exact identity binding prevents false lifecycle promotion when a
  relevant verification input changes.
- Null hypothesis: the identity is insufficient to distinguish stale from current
  evidence, or rejects unchanged scope unpredictably.
- One independent variable: result-only acceptance versus current-identity-bound
  receipt acceptance.
- Fixture set: fresh pass; missing or failed receipt; source, verifier,
  arguments, config, runtime, and lockfile change; tampered body; unchanged
  scope; replay.
- Primary metric: false lifecycle promotions.
- Safety-critical counter: false promotions must be zero for treatment.
- Required access: local Go toolchain only.

The minimal gate is small enough to test deterministically and addresses a named

## Taste update

The useful part of this paper is not its autonomous-loop outcome. It makes the
causal boundary explicit: a lifecycle must act on independently admissible
evidence, not agent text. This suggests a screening rule for future papers:
prefer designs that reveal the invalidation key and the failure path over designs
that report a successful agent run.
