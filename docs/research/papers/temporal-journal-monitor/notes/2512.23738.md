Final disposition: experiment-candidate

## Narrow claim

The abstract claims that runtime enforcement of formal temporal properties can
prevent an agent from emitting unsafe tool-call sequences. Its reported results
are not evidence that a particular swe-term rule set is complete or sufficient.

## Mechanism

- Treatment: check the sequence of proposed actions against temporal safety
  properties and block non-compliant actions.
- Control: unrestricted or conventional guardrail behavior.
- Enforcement point: action generation/runtime boundary.
- Dependencies: a domain-specific language, first-order logic translation, SMT
  solving, and constrained generation.

## Evidence

- Fixtures, dataset, or benchmark: retail and airline tool-use applications
  (abstract-level read; the specific benchmark suites were not confirmed).
- Repetitions and sample size: not recorded from the abstract; unknown.
- Primary and component metrics: conformance to the stated temporal
  properties; the abstract reports perfect conformance in its setup.
- Ablations or counterfactuals: unknown; not established at abstract level.
- Discordant or negative cases: none reported in the abstract, which is itself
  a reason to treat the result as domain-bound.
- Result most likely to change our mind: a legal action sequence the enforcer
  wrongly blocks, or an unsafe sequence the properties cannot express.

## Evaluator audit and limits

- Measurement type: deterministic conformance checking of action sequences,
  over model-generated trajectories.
- Independence from the system under test: the enforcer is the system under
  test; conformance is measured by the same property set it enforces.
- Rubric, judge, or verifier identity: the paper's temporal property set and
  solver; not transferable as-is.
- Known disagreement, variance, or circularity: the paper evaluates retail and
  airline applications across multiple models. The abstract reports perfect
  conformance in its setup, but that is a model- and domain-bound result. This
  local experiment avoids model-judged outcomes and measures only deterministic
  trace acceptance/rejection.

## Boundary conditions and limitations

The paper's claim holds for properties that its DSL can express and its solver
can decide within the action loop; it says nothing about properties outside
that language or about enforcement cost at scale. Inference: the transferable
part is the enforcement boundary (check ordering before an action takes
effect), not the property language. A closed rule set can miss a transition the
paper's language would have expressed, and the local experiment must treat that
as a live null, not an accepted cost.

## swe-term transfer

- Observed local failure: the target control plane has no mechanical check
  that approval precedes a governed mutation, that one lease is active, that
  observed effects stay within their declaration, or that a receipt is current
  before a lifecycle claim.
- Relevant contracts: target `ControlEvent`, `ControlJournal`,
  `MutationLease`, and invariants 2–4 and 6.
- Smallest transplantable mechanism: a deterministic monitor over a closed,
  versioned event vocabulary.
- What not to copy: generated temporal policy, SMT solver, constrained decoding,
  and the retail/airline environments.
- New risk or failure mode introduced: a closed vocabulary that omits a needed
  transition fails closed on legal work; unbounded monitor state would make
  replay non-deterministic.

## Cheapest falsification

- Hypothesis: a small monitor over closed, versioned events can reject each
  frozen unsafe ordering trace with a stable rule ID while accepting legal
  traces and reaching the same state after prefix replay.
- Null hypothesis: the fixed vocabulary misses a required transition, accepts
  an illegal trace, rejects a legal trace, has replay-sensitive state, or
  requires unbounded resident state.
- One independent variable: outcome-only control acceptance versus
  deterministic event ordering and receipt-target checks in the treatment
  reducer.
- Smallest fixture set or deterministic trace: legal and illegal synthetic
  event traces, duplicates, sequence gaps, cancellation, and prefix replay
  under a pure Go reducer.
- Primary component metric: illegal treatment traces accepted.
- Safety-critical counter that may not be averaged away: unsafe lifecycle
  promotions must be zero.
- Required model, compute, artifact, or access: local Go toolchain only.

## Qualitative judgment

- Transfer relevance: high
- Evidence strength: unknown (abstract-level read; sample sizes and ablations

- Evaluator validity: medium (deterministic conformance, but circular with the

- Reproduction feasibility: high for the local reducer; low for the paper's

- Operational fit: high for a closed reducer; low for the DSL and solver
- Independent support: low (Progent and AgentSpec support runtime enforcement
  generally but are reference-only here)

## Disposition

- [x] experiment-candidate

The useful transfer is a narrow, independently testable enforcement boundary;
the paper's formal machinery is deliberately excluded.

## Taste update

The paper makes the enforcement point precise: ordering is checked before an
action takes effect, not judged afterwards. What looked persuasive and did not
survive the read is the property language itself; a general DSL would add a
second policy surface that a model could author at runtime. Future screening
should prefer papers that expose the enforcement boundary and failure path
over papers that report perfect conformance on their own property set.
