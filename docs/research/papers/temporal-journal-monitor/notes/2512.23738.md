## Narrow claim

The abstract claims that runtime enforcement of formal temporal properties can
prevent an agent from emitting unsafe tool-call sequences. Its reported results
are not evidence that a particular swe-term rule set is complete or sufficient.

## Mechanism

- Treatment: check the sequence of proposed actions against temporal safety
  properties and block non-compliant actions.
- Control: unrestricted or conventional guardrail behavior.
- Enforcement point: action generation/runtime boundary.
- Dependencies: a domain-specific language, first-order logic translation, SMT
  solving, and constrained generation.

## Evaluator audit and limits

The paper evaluates retail and airline applications across multiple models. The
abstract reports perfect conformance in its setup, but that is a model- and
domain-bound result. This local experiment avoids model judged outcomes and
measures only deterministic trace acceptance/rejection.

## swe-term transfer

- Relevant contracts: target `ControlEvent`, `ControlJournal`,
  `MutationLease`, and invariants 2–4 and 6.
- Smallest transplantable mechanism: a deterministic monitor over a closed,
  versioned event vocabulary.
- What not to copy: generated temporal policy, SMT solver, constrained decoding,
  and the retail/airline environments.
- Cheapest falsification: legal and illegal synthetic event traces, replay, and
  cancellation under a pure Go reducer.
